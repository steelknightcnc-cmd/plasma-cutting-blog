Five variables

What controls cut quality

01

Amperage

Match amperage to the installed consumables and material thickness. Too little power usually forces an excessively slow cut.

02

Cutting speed

Speed strongly affects dross and bevel. Tune it in small steps after confirming the torch, air, and consumables are correct.

03

Air supply

Pressure must remain stable while cutting. Clean, dry air and adequate sustained flow matter more than tank pressure alone.

04

Standoff

Hold the torch at the correct cut height and use a higher pierce height when required to reduce blowback damage.

05

Consumables

A damaged nozzle or deeply pitted electrode can ruin a cut even when every numerical setting is correct.

Shop-floor diagnosis

Read the cut before changing settings

Heavy removable bottom dross

Usually points to a cut that is too slow. Increase speed gradually and retest.

Top spatter or incomplete penetration

Often means the torch is moving too fast, the amperage is too low, or the air supply is unstable.

Excessive bevel

Check torch squareness, standoff, consumable condition, cut direction, and machine motion before changing amperage.

Rough or wandering cut face

Inspect the nozzle and electrode, then verify dry air, stable pressure, correct height, and smooth motion.
